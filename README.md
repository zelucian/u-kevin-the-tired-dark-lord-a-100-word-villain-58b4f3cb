A 100-word monologue spoken by Malachar, Keeper of the Ledger of the Dead — a lich who meant to conquer a kingdom by winter, but the paperwork got in the way. The tiredness lives in the rhythm, the pauses, and the small deflections, never in a plain statement of weariness.

## The monologue

> Come in, hero. Mind the paperwork. I am Malachar, Keeper of the Ledger of the Dead, and your soul is number four thousand, two hundred and— hold on. Let me find the page. Yes. Here. You died bravely. The forms will say so. Sign here, and here, and— oh, the ink has dried again. The candles burn low, the dead keep arriving, and the queue outside my crypt stretches past the marshes. I meant to conquer your kingdom by winter. Winter came. Then spring. Perhaps... tomorrow I shall raise the army. Tonight, the ledger stays open, and so do I.

## Run it

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

This writes the monologue to `out/entry.txt` (or `/output/entry.txt` when `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` is unset). The program validates the text before writing: 1–600 characters and 90–110 words, throwing otherwise. It is deterministic, uses no network and no randomness — two runs produce byte-identical output.
